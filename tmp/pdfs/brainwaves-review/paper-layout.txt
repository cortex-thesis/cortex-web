                                               Definition of Terms


Adaptive Multimedia Learning Environment. A digital instructional system that dynamically

modifies    the   presentation,     pacing,    or  modality     of  learning    content    in  response    to  learner

characteristics,    behaviors,     or  detected    cognitive     states.  In  this  study,   it  refers   to  Cortex's

stimulus-driven instructional delivery framework.


Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity              Disorder       (ADHD).        A     neurodevelopmental           disorder

characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, impulsivity, and/or hyperactivity that interfere

with   academic,     behavioral,    and    social  functioning.     The   study    focuses   on   learners   formally

identified with ADHD within Special Education settings.


Brain–Computer          Interface     (BCI).    A   system    that  enables    communication        between     neural

activity  and   computer      applications    through    the  acquisition,    processing,     and  interpretation    of

brain  signals.   In  this  study,  EEG     serves   as  the  primary    BCI   mechanism       supporting    adaptive

instructional responses.


Brainwave       Frequency       Bands.     Distinct   ranges    of   EEG     activity   measured      in  hertz   (Hz),

including    theta,  alpha,  beta,   and   gamma     oscillations.   These    frequency     bands   are  analyzed    as

indicators of learner attention, arousal, and cognitive engagement.


Cognitive     State   Classification.     The   computational      process    of  identifying    a  learner's  current

mental condition based on extracted EEG features. The study classifies learners into four states:

Focused, Distracted, Relaxed, and Overstimulated.

Consumer-Grade          EEG Device.       A portable,    non-invasive wearable EEG            headset designed for

real-time   brainwave      acquisition    outside   clinical   environments.      Within    this  study,   the  device

Cortex. The proposed EEG-integrated neuro-adaptive multimedia application developed in this

Dynamic      Clustering      Model.     A   machine-learning       classification    approach    that   continuously

Electroencephalography (EEG). A non-invasive neurophysiological monitoring technique that

supersede    the   system's    automated      adaptive    responses    when     pedagogical     judgment      requires

immediate intervention.


Neuro-Adaptive        Learning      System.     A  learning    environment      that  utilizes  neurophysiological

information,     particularly    EEG     signals,   to  modify     instructional    experiences     according     to  a

learner's real-time cognitive condition.

                                                   Introduction

         Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity          Disorder      (ADHD)        is  among      the    most    prevalent

neurodevelopmental        disorders    affecting   children and adolescents worldwide.            Characterized by

persistent   patterns    of   inattention,   hyperactivity,     and    impulsivity,    ADHD       can   significantly

influence   learners' academic performance, classroom behavior, and daily functioning. Although

commonly classified into predominantly              inattentive,   predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and

combined     presentations,     the  condition    affects   each   learner   differently,  requiring    instructional

approaches that accommodate varying cognitive and behavioral needs (Magnus et al., 2023).

         Within     the   Philippine      educational     system,     Special     Education      (SPED)      provides

individualized     support    for  learners    with   disabilities,   including    those   with   ADHD.      Through

learner-centered instructional approaches and the Transition Curriculum Framework, SPED aims

to develop    not  only academic competencies but            also   functional,   social, and independent living

skills that  prepare    learners   for  meaningful     participation in     school   and  society (Pawilen et al.,

2018).   Achieving     these   goals,  however,     remains    challenging     for  learners   with   ADHD whose

attention and engagement often fluctuate throughout instruction.

         To   address    these    challenges,     educators     increasingly     integrate    multimedia      learning

materials   that  combine     visual,   auditory,   and   interactive   elements    to promote     engagement and

improve    learning    experiences.     At   the  same    time,   advances     in  educational    technology      have

introduced    opportunities     to make    instructional    delivery   more    responsive to learners'      changing

cognitive    states   through    emerging      technologies     such    as  electroencephalography          (EEG),    a

non-invasive method for monitoring brain activity.

         Despite these developments, current multimedia learning systems remain unable to adapt

instructional    content    based    on   the  learner’s    real-time   cognitive     state.  This   gap   limits   the

effectiveness    of  digital  interventions     for  learners who experience         rapid   cognitive fluctuations,

such  as those with ADHD. To address this limitation, there is a critical need to investigate how

Curriculum,      dynamically      aligning     digital   content    with    real-time    cognitive     metrics    while

Background of the Study

         Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity          Disorder     (ADHD)       is  a   neurodevelopmental         disorder

characterized    by   persistent   patterns   of inattention,    hyperactivity, and      impulsivity that      interfere

with learning, behavior, and daily functioning (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). Within

educational    settings,   these   characteristics     often  make     it difficult  for  learners   with   ADHD       to

sustain   attention,   regulate    behavior,     and   complete     instructional    activities   without    additional

support.   As   a result,  many    educational     systems have adopted         learner-centered     approaches that

provide individualized instruction based on the specific needs of students.

         In the Philippines, Special Education (SPED) programs aim to provide equitable learning

opportunities for     learners with disabilities      through differentiated instruction and individualized

educational     planning.    Under     DepEd     Order    No.   021,   s.  2020,    learners   with   disabilities   are

supported     through    the   K–12    Transition     Curriculum,     which    emphasizes      the   development      of

functional    academics,     independent      living,   communication,        career   awareness,     and   vocational

skills. Rather than following a fixed subject-based curriculum, the Transition Curriculum focuses

on  preparing learners      for independent      participation    in school, work, home, and community life






                                                            5

===== PDF PAGE 12 =====
through Individualized Transition Plans (ITPs) tailored to each learner's goals and developmental

needs.

         The    researchers    conducted      a  preliminary     needs    assessment     at  Panorama      Montessori

School Inc. (PMSI),        where teachers reported         that maintaining the attention and engagement of

learners   with   ADHD       remains    one   of  the   most   common       instructional    challenges.    Classroom

observations    and teacher     feedback indicated       that learners often experience fluctuating levels of

attention   throughout      instructional     sessions,    requiring    teachers    to   continuously     adjust    their

teaching    strategies.   Survey    findings    further   revealed    that  most    learners   are  able   to maintain

sustained attention for approximately 10 to 15 minutes before requiring instructional variation or

re-engagement activities. While          teachers    effectively employ classroom management techniques

and differentiated instruction, these interventions are primarily based on behavioral observation

rather than objective indicators of learners' cognitive states.

         One challenge identified in current instructional practice is that learners' cognitive states

are largely invisible during classroom instruction. Teachers can observe external behaviors such

as movement, silence, or participation; however, these behaviors do not always accurately reflect

whether    a  learner   is  focused,    distracted,   mentally     fatigued,   or  overstimulated.      Consequently,

instructional   adjustments     often   occur only after disengagement has already become observable,

limiting opportunities for timely intervention.

         Another      challenge     involves     the   limitations     of   conventional       multimedia      learning

applications. Although educational multimedia has been shown to improve learner engagement,

most existing applications present instructional materials using a fixed sequence regardless of the

learner's current attentional or cognitive condition. For learners with ADHD, whose engagement

naturally   fluctuates    throughout     instruction,    static  multimedia      may    be  insufficient    to  support

sustained   participation     because    the  instructional presentation       cannot adapt     to changing     learner

needs in real time.


         Teachers also have limited access to objective information regarding learner engagement

during instructional sessions. Current monitoring methods primarily depend on direct classroom

observation     and   post-activity    assessment,      making     it difficult   to  identify    subtle   changes    in

attention   while   learning    activities  are  still in  progress.    This   limits  teachers'   ability   to deliver

timely   interventions     or   modify    instructional    strategies    according    to  the   learner's   immediate

condition.


         Recent     developments       in   wearable      Electroencephalography          (EEG),      brain-computer

interfaces,    and   adaptive     educational     technologies      provide     opportunities      to  address     these

limitations. EEG is a non-invasive technology capable of measuring electrical activity from the

brain  through     scalp-mounted       sensors.   Advances      in portable    dry-electrode     EEG    devices    have

enabled    their   application     beyond     clinical   environments,       supporting     research    in  education,

cognitive    monitoring,      and    adaptive     learning    systems.     Combined       with    machine      learning

techniques,     EEG     signals   may    be   used    to  infer  cognitive     states  associated     with   attention,

relaxation,   distraction,   and   cognitive    workload,    allowing educational        systems to respond        more

dynamically to learner needs.


         Despite these technological advancements, existing adaptive learning systems continue to

present   important    limitations.   Many systems rely solely on behavioral indicators, operate using

predetermined       instructional     sequences,      or   lack    teacher-centered       monitoring      capabilities.

Furthermore,      relatively    few    systems     combine      real-time    EEG     monitoring,      neuro-adaptive

multimedia      presentation,     and    teacher     supervision     within     Special    Education      classrooms

implementing the Philippine Transition Curriculum.


         To      address      these      gaps,      this     study       proposes       Cortex,       an     assistive

Electroencephalogram-integrated Neuro-Adaptive Multimedia Application designed for learners

with ADHD within Transition Curriculum classrooms. Cortex utilizes wearable EEG technology

to classify learner cognitive states in real time and dynamically presents appropriate multimedia

templates     corresponding       to   Focused,     Distracted,     Relaxed,     and    Overstimulated       learning

conditions.   Simultaneously, the       system provides teachers with a live monitoring dashboard that

supports    informed      instructional    decisions     while    allowing     manual     classroom     control.    By

integrating   neurophysiological       monitoring     with   adaptive multimedia learning, Cortex seeks              to

supplement     existing   instructional    practices   and   contribute    to  more   responsive, individualized,

and technology-assisted learning experiences for learners with ADHD.


         Within   this  context,   three  persistent and interrelated problems limit the effectiveness of

learning environments for students with ADHD in Philippine SpEd classrooms.

         First,  the cognitive     states   of students with ADHD remain difficult to identify using

conventional observation-based approaches, as fluctuations in attention, engagement, and

cognitive   activity are often       not directly visible during learning sessions. Teachers primarily

rely on behavioral cues such as movement, eye contact, and classroom participation to determine

whether    a   learner   is  attentive   or  distracted.    However,     research    shows     that  ADHD-related

attention   fluctuates   because     of  underlying     neurophysiological       processes    that  are   not  always

reflected  through    visible behavior (Strauß, 2018; Wang et al., 2024). As a result, teachers often

recognize    disengagement       only  after  it has already affected learning,         limiting opportunities for

timely intervention. Findings from the researchers' preliminary survey at PMSI likewise indicate


                                                           8

===== PDF PAGE 15 =====
that teachers    depend largely on       behavioral    observation and manual         classroom     strategies   when

monitoring learner engagement.

         Second,    existing    multimedia       learning    environments       often    provide    static  learning

experiences that do        not  adapt    to the   changing cognitive and attentional states of students

with ADHD, potentially limiting sustained engagement and effective learning participation.

Although     multimedia      supports     learner   engagement,       most    educational     applications    present

content at a fixed pace       regardless of    the learner's current attentional condition. Studies suggest

that learners with ADHD benefit from instructional materials that can adjust to changing levels

of attention,   cognitive load, and sensory stimulation            (Frolli  et al., 2023; Hulsbosch, 2025).          In

local   classrooms,     teachers    compensate       by   manually     modifying      activities   and   introducing

additional   visual   or  auditory    stimuli   to  regain   learner   attention,   highlighting     the  absence    of

adaptive instructional technologies.

         Third, teachers have limited access to objective and real-time information regarding

the cognitive state-based engagement levels of students with ADHD, making it challenging

to   provide     timely     instructional       support      and    personalized        learning     interventions.

Instructional   decisions    are often based on observation, experience, and post-activity assessment

rather   than    continuous     evidence      of  learners'    cognitive    conditions.     While     Individualized

Transition    Plans    (ITPs)    guide    instructional    planning,     meaningful      personalization      remains

challenging    without    objective    information     regarding    learners'   moment-to-moment          attentional

states (Mangonon, 2022;         Reyes,   2023). This limitation becomes           particularly   significant   within

the Transition Curriculum, where learners are expected to sustain engagement across functional,

vocational, and daily living activities.

         Recent    advances     in  wearable     electroencephalography          (EEG),    machine     learning,    and

adaptive     educational      technologies       present    opportunities       to   address     these    challenges.

Consumer-grade        EEG      devices    now     allow    non-invasive      monitoring      of   brain   activity    in

educational settings, while       intelligent   classification    techniques make it possible to infer learner

attention   in real  time.   At   the  same time, adaptive multimedia           systems have demonstrated the

potential to personalize instructional delivery according to learners' cognitive conditions.

         Fourth,    there   are   only limited     existing system      that   integrate real-time EEG-based

cognitive state classification, adaptive multimedia delivery, and teacher monitoring support

within   a  single   framework designed           for Special Education         learners under the        Transition

Curriculum.      While    individual components         such as    EEG classifiers,     adaptive    content   engines,

and learning dashboards have been explored in separate research contexts, their integration into a

unified,   classroom-deployable         system     remains    absent    in  the   literature,   particularly    within

Philippine    SPED     environments.      This   gap   means     that  even   where    component-level       solutions

exist, they   cannot be applied       in combination to provide simultaneous cognitive state visibility,

instructional responsiveness, and teacher oversight within the same instructional session.

         The   absence     of  such    an  integrated    framework      carries   an   additional   implication:     no

established    evaluation    standard    currently    exists   by  which    the  combined      pipeline,   from   EEG

preprocessing     through    cognitive    state   classification    to  adaptive   content    delivery   and   teacher

dashboard response, can be assessed as a unified whole rather than as a collection of independent

components.       Individual      component       metrics     such     as   classification      accuracy,     filtering

effectiveness,    or   usability    scores    capture    performance       within    a  single    layer   but   cannot

characterize    whether the pipeline as        a system achieves its stated educational purpose: reducing

the  latency   and   inaccuracy    of  instructional    response    for ADHD learners         under   the  Transition

Curriculum. This evaluative gap means that even a system in which every individual component

performs    adequately     could   still fail  to deliver   coherent, timely,      and  pedagogically appropriate

         The   four identified    challenges: (1)     the  limited visibility of     learners'   real-time cognitive

states,  (2)  the  rigidity of   existing multimedia       learning environments, (3)         the  lack of    objective

data to support instructional decisions highlight the need for a more adaptive educational system,

(4) the absence of a unified framework and evaluation standard for neuro-adaptive SPED tools,

highlight the urgent need for a more adaptive educational system. Recent advances in wearable

electroencephalography          (EEG),      machine      learning,    and    neuro-adaptive       multimedia       have

demonstrated      the   feasibility   of   monitoring     learner    attention    in  real  time   and    dynamically

responding     to  changing    cognitive conditions       (García-Moreno et al., 2025; Zhang et al.,              2025;

lack  of  a  real-time    EEG-based       system    capable    of detecting    the  changing     cognitive    states   of

students with ADHD, adapting multimedia instruction accordingly, and providing teachers with

objective cognitive-state data to support timely and individualized instructional decision-making

within   Special Education       settings. To address this need, the study proposes Cortex, an assistive

EEG-integrated       neuro-adaptive       multimedia      application    designed     to  support    stimulus-driven

learning    among     students    with    ADHD       under    the   Transition    Curriculum.      By    continuously

classifying learners' cognitive states, presenting appropriate multimedia templates, and providing

Objectives of the Study

         The       study's       main       objective        is     to      elevate       processes        involving

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity        Disorder (ADHD) learning management by developing Cortex,

an   assistive    electroencephalogram-integrated             neuro-adaptive       multimedia      application     on

transition   curriculum    framework,     that utilizes real-time EEG-based brainwave classification to

detect   and    respond    to   the   cognitive    states   of  students     with   ADHD,      enabling     adaptive

stimulus-driven learning experiences while providing teachers with objective, real-time cognitive

state monitoring     tools to support individualized instructional intervention in Special Education

settings.

         The specific objectives are as follows:

    1.   To  improve      the  identification     of  cognitive    states  among     students     with   ADHD      by

         developing      a  dynamic      EEG-based        brainwave       classifier   using    classification    via

         clustering    technique,     evaluated     against    a  baseline    of  a  static,  generalized     global

         classifier   and   a  literature-standard        Theta-Beta      Ratio   (TBR)     threshold     model,    to

         serve   as   real-time    indicators     of  attention     and   engagement       during     instructional

         sessions.


                                                          12

===== PDF PAGE 19 =====
         The study will develop an EEG-based cognitive state detection system that continuously

         captures    brainwave      signals    from    students    with   ADHD       through    a   consumer-grade

         wearable EEG device         during    instructional sessions. Raw EEG signals will be processed

         through     a   preprocessing       pipeline    that    filters   movement       artifacts    and    extracts

         frequency-band       features,   specifically    theta,   beta,  alpha,   and    gamma     power     spectral

         values,   which     serve   as  the   primary    neurophysiological        indicators    of  cognitive    and

         attentional   states.  These    features    will  be  input   into   a  dynamic    clustering    model    that

         categorizes    each student's real-time brainwave           activity into one of four target cognitive

         states:  focused,    relaxed,    distracted,   or  overstimulated.      Rather    than  relying    on  static,

         pre-labeled    training   data   alone,   the  clustering   approach     allows   the  system    to  identify

         naturally   occurring groupings       in each    student's  brainwave patterns,       accommodating the

         neurological    heterogeneity      characteristic   of  ADHD       learners   whose    attentional   profiles

         differ significantly across individuals. The classified states will be updated continuously

         throughout    each session, providing an objective, real-time data stream that reflects each

         student's moment-to-moment cognitive condition, replacing observational inference with

         neurophysiologically grounded state identification.

    2.   To  assist   and   support     the  learning    experience      of  students    with   ADHD through          a

         neuro-adaptive,       stimulus-driven      multimedia environment             that  dynamically adjusts

         instructional     content    in  response     to  each   student's    EEG-classified       cognitive state,

         promoting      sustained     attention     and    participation     within    a  transition     curriculum

         context through focus and relax mode delivery.

         The   study   will  develop    a  student-facing     adaptive    multimedia application that        receives

         EEG-classified      cognitive    state  data   and   responds    by   dynamically      adjusting   the  type,

Rationale of the Study


         In the global pursuit of equitable development, United Nations Sustainable Development

Goal 4 (SDG 4) mandates member-states to ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and

promote    lifelong   learning   opportunities for     all. Specifically, in SDG Target 4.5, the proponents

aim   to   support    equal    access    to  education     and   building     independence       among     vulnerable

populations,    explicitly   for persons with disabilities, measured structurally by Indicator 4.5.1. In

the  Philippine     educational     landscape,     this  macro-directive       is  localized    via  Department       of

Education (DepEd) Order No. 021, s. 2020, which establishes the K to 12 Transition Curriculum

Framework for Learners with Disabilities (LWDs). This policy focuses on crucial life paths such

as  functional     life  paths,   entrepreneurship,       and   employment       readiness     through    specialized

instructional    frameworks      like   Care   Skills   and   Functional     Academics.      However,      traditional,

uniform digital and manual instructional pacing in Special Education (SPED) centers frequently

hits immediate bottlenecks         when accommodating learners            with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity

Disorder    (ADHD)       due   to  severe   processing     delays,    varying    attention   tracks,   and   cognitive

overload.




                                                           17

===== PDF PAGE 24 =====
         While standard computer- or tablet-based setups offer localized accessibility, they operate

as static,  open-loop     delivery    platforms    that  lack real-time     visibility  into the user’s fluctuating

cognitive    states.  When     an  ADHD       student    experiences     overstimulation      or  severe   distraction,

standard     systems     continue     streaming     content     uniformly,     exacerbating       cognitive     fatigue,

disengagement, or behavioral escalation. Manual classroom interventions depend heavily on the

continuous     physical    vigilance    of   SPED     teachers,    who    must    observe    behavioral     shifts  and

manually alter materials to keep the room stable. Consequently, there is an urgent technical and

pedagogical gap for assistive technology capable of intercepting these internal cognitive shifts in

real-time, bridging the gap between physiological strain and automated educational adjustments

within the bounds of the mandated local curriculum.


         To directly address this gap, this study proposes the development of Cortex, an assistive,

neuro-adaptive multimedia application integrated with electroencephalogram (EEG) technology.

By capturing     real-time    bio-signals    through   a non-invasive      wireless    EEG    headband, the system

interprets raw    frequency band fluctuations into classified cognitive states, specifically Focused,

Relaxed,    Distracted,    and   Overstimulated.       The   software    directly   closes   the  feedback     loop   by

driving   dynamic     multimedia      pacing,    focus   prompts,     and  calming     adaptations    tailored   to  the

competencies      of  the  DepEd     Transition    Curriculum.      While    this research may not attain 100%

mitigation of the biological symptoms associated with ADHD, it provides an algorithmic safety

net that systematically buffers cognitive overload, fosters self-regulation, and stabilizes executive

functioning during learning sessions.


         The   general    implementation       of  Cortex    offers   an  immediate,      tangible   benefit   to  local

SPED     locales   and   centers   by   acting   as  an   objective,    data-driven    assistant.   It  optimizes    the

instructional   process by     automating sensory        regulation and pacing, thereby significantly easing


                                                           18

===== PDF PAGE 25 =====
the cognitive    and   administrative     monitoring workloads of SPED educators. Rather than relying

on  subjective behavioral logging, teachers are equipped with a descriptive analytics engine and

post-session    reporting    tools   that  quantify     how    cognitive    states  map    to  specific   curriculum

packages.    Ultimately, by     transforming standard digital instructional           material    into  a responsive,

neuro-adaptive     learning   environment,      Cortex enhances       session   productivity and accelerates the

path of ADHD learners toward mastering independent life and career competencies.


Scope and Delimitation

         This   study    focuses    on   the   design    and   development       of  Cortex,    an  EEG-integrated
neuro-adaptive         multimedia         application        intended        to      support       learners       with
Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity         Disorder    (ADHD)       within   Special    Education     (SPED)     settings.
The    system      utilizes   real-time     electroencephalogram          (EEG)      signals    obtained      from    a
consumer-grade       wearable     EEG    headset    to  classify   learner   cognitive    states   and   dynamically
adjust multimedia instructional delivery during transition curriculum activities.

         The   conceptual     framework      for  this  study begins     by identifying     three converging input

categories    that  establish    the   foundation     for  Cortex:     the  intended     beneficiaries,    the   current

solutions already in      use, and the technological         opportunities that make a new approach viable.

The   beneficiaries identified      are SPED      centers and educators,       students    within   the  target school

locale,  and ADHD        students and individuals specifically, a layering that establishes Cortex as a

system designed not for         ADHD      students   in isolation, but for the entire instructional ecosystem

surrounding them, including the teachers who must adapt lessons in real time and the institutions

that  must    support    the   necessary     infrastructure.    The    current   solutions    documented,       namely


                                                           26

===== PDF PAGE 33 =====
behavioral    modification plans and token economy                systems, pre-made visual aids and recorded

instructional    videos,   and periodic teacher observation           paired with anecdotal        progress    tracking,

reflect  practices consistent with what classroom practitioners actually rely on today, confirming

that instructional adjustment in this space remains manual, reactive, and dependent on a teacher's

subjective   interpretation     rather than any objective        or continuous measure. Alongside these, the

opportunities      identified,     EEG       as   an    increasingly       accessible      neuro-monitoring         tool,

neuro-adaptive systems as an emerging technological category, and real-time adaptive stimuli as

a feasible    delivery   mechanism,       establish   why    an  EEG-based       intervention    is  both   timely   and

technically achievable rather than purely theoretical.


         These    three   input   categories     converge     into   three   clearly   defined    problems      that  the

framework      positions   Cortex    to  resolve.   The    first is that  ADHD students         struggle to    maintain

optimal cognitive states during learning, which reflects the core behavioral reality underlying the

study. The second is that no real-time system currently exists to detect and respond to cognitive

state  changes     as  they   occur,   representing     the  technological     gap   left  unaddressed     by   existing

classroom     practices.   The    third  is  that  cognitive    and   learning    state  shifts  in  ADHD       students

consequently go undetected, resulting in delayed or entirely absent instructional response, since

teachers can only react once a student has already visibly disengaged or become overstimulated.

Each of these problems is met with a corresponding solution that forms a direct bridge between

diagnosis    and    design.    The    first  solution    proposes    the   development       of  an   EEG-integrated

cognitive    state  classifier   using   dynamic      clustering    to  distinguish    states  such   as  focused    and

distracted,   replacing    subjective teacher      judgment with an objective,          signal-based classification

layer.  The    second     proposes     the   construction     of   an   adaptive    multimedia       content    delivery

mechanism       that  adjusts    the  type   of   stimulus    presented     based    on   the  detected    brain   state,

Review of Related Literature


Special Education (SpEd) and Transition Curriculum


         Philippine     Special    Education      (SpEd)    is  grounded      in  a   legislative   framework       that

mandates individualized, functional           instruction for    learners with disabilities.      Republic     Act No.

7277,   or  the   Magna     Carta   for  Persons    with   Disabilities,    and   Republic     Act   No.   10533,    the

Enhanced     Basic Education Act of         2013, collectively      establish that    education    for learners    with

disabilities   must    extend    beyond    academic      content    toward    developing      independence,      social

participation,   and   productive     functioning     in society   (Reyes,    2023).    In  the  Philippine    context,

DepEd (2020, p. 3) affirms that the primary goal of SpEd is "for all learners with disabilities to


                                                           29

===== PDF PAGE 36 =====
become     self-reliant,  purposeful,     and  productive     members      of  their  families,   communities,      and

society." SpEd thus functions not merely as a remedial framework but as an equity-driven system

that aims to develop the fullest possible potential of every learner regardless of disability type or

severity.


         Overview of Special Education. Despite this legislative foundation, the implementation

of  SpEd    programs     across    Philippine    schools    remains    structurally    constrained.    Reyes    (2023)

identifies   persistent   gaps    in  trained   personnel,     adequate    assessment      tools,  and   instructional

flexibility,  while    Espeño     et   al.  (2024)    document      curriculum      inflexibility   and    insufficient

differentiated resources as the most frequently reported obstacles to effective SpEd delivery. The

primary    instrument    intended    to bridge    policy and practice is the Individualized Transition Plan

(ITP), defined by DepEd (2020, p. 4) as "the educational plan used for mapping out short-term to

long-term outcomes from which annual goals and objectives are defined." In practice, however,

Mangonon      (2022)    finds   that ITPs   are frequently     generic    rather than genuinely individualized,

partly  because teachers have no          reliable mechanism       for generating the real-time observational

data  that  meaningful      individualization     requires.   Within    this  system, DepEd Order         No.   021, s.

2020   formalizes the K to 12        Transition Curriculum Framework              for Learners     with   Disabilities,

organizing    instruction    into   seven   learning    packages:     Care,   Life,  Pre-Vocational,      Livelihood,

Functional Academics, Career, and Enrichment. These packages constitute the curricular basis of

the  multimedia       content    integrated    into   Cortex    and    validated    through     the  study's    partner

institution.

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and Learning Characteristics


         Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity          Disorder     (ADHD)       is  a  neurodevelopmental         disorder

characterized     by  persistent    patterns   of  inattention,   impulsivity,    and   hyperactivity     that  impact

cognitive,   behavioral,    academic, and       social functioning across       multiple settings (Soreff,       2022;

Núñez-Jaramillo        et  al.,  2021).     The    DSM-5      classifies    ADHD       into   three    presentations:

predominantly       inattentive,    predominantly       hyperactive-impulsive,         and    combined.      Globally,

within   the   Philippine    educational     context,    Zabala    Jr. and    Manabat     (2017)    documented       its

behavioral     manifestations      among     Grade     I  learners,    noting    that  teachers    addressed      these

challenges with limited systemic support. Critically, the neurobiological heterogeneity of ADHD

means    that  behavioral     observation     alone   is insufficient    for  understanding      individual    learner

states,  highlighting     the  value   of  physiological      monitoring     as  a  complementary        approach     to

detecting cognitive conditions in educational settings.


         Learning Difficulties of ADHD Students. Learning difficulties in ADHD are not fixed

capacity   deficits   but  dynamic,     load-sensitive     impairments      that  fluctuate    in  response    to  task

demands     and affective conditions.       Thomson      (2020) demonstrated that         ADHD learners        display

significantly    higher   response     time   variability    and   intermittent    attentional    lapses   relative   to

typically developing peers, while Wagner et al. (2024) found that cognitive fatigue and negative

affect erode behavioral engagement well before overt disengagement becomes observable. Parks

(2021)   confirmed      that  comprehension       performance      in  ADHD       improves     when    cognitive   and

structural   demands     are  reduced,    and   Morsink     et al. (2020)    found that    cognitively demanding

tasks  are   perceived     as  less  motivating,     with   motivational     salience    playing    a direct   role  in

engagement      variability.   In  the  Philippine    context,   Tulo    and  San   Jose   (2026)    highlighted    the

sustained    manual    differentiation    demands      placed   on   teachers    working    with   ADHD      learners,

while   Lukban     et al. (2024)    found    that structured    motivational     scaffolding was more effective

than  purely    directive   approaches     in  sustaining    academic     engagement      among     Filipino   ADHD

learners.   Collectively,    these   findings    establish   that   static  instructional    formats    have   limited

capacity   to  respond     to  the  fluctuating    cognitive    and   motivational     states  of  ADHD      learners,

reinforcing the need for adaptive, real-time responsive delivery.

EEG Preprocessing and State Classification Techniques


         Translating raw      brainwave signals into reliable           cognitive   state classifications requires a

structured      pipeline     spanning      signal     preprocessing,        feature     extraction,     and     machine

learning-based classification. Each stage addresses a specific challenge in extracting meaningful



                                                            35

===== PDF PAGE 42 =====
neural information from continuous EEG data, and each is directly implemented in Cortex's data

collection and modeling        phase.   This   theme reviews the techniques underpinning                that pipeline,

with focus on the methods Cortex adopts and the evidence supporting their suitability for ADHD

educational contexts.


EEG Signal       Processing.     Raw EEG signals        are highly sensitive to noise and interference, with

artifacts  from    eye  blinks,   facial   muscle    movement,      body    movement,       and  external    electrical

interference contributing up to 30 to 40% of unwanted signal distortion if not properly removed

(Jiang et al., 2019). Preprocessing is therefore a critical precondition for accurate classification.

Band-pass      filtering   retains   EEG     frequencies     within    the   cognitively     relevant   range    while

removing irrelevant low- and high-frequency signals, with most systems applying a range of 0.5

to 50  Hz to preserve essential neural information (Yahya et al., 2019). Notch filtering at 50 Hz

eliminates    power-line    interference    specific   to  the  Philippine    electrical   standard    (Leske    et al.,

2019).   Independent      Component       Analysis     (ICA)    separates    ocular   and   muscle     artifacts  from

genuine    brain  signals   (Singh    et  al., 2017),   and   epoching    segments     the  continuous signal into

fixed-length     windows      suitable   for   feature   extraction.    In   ADHD-related        EEG     studies,   this

preprocessing     pipeline   is especially    critical: children with ADHD are more prone to movement

and  fidgeting    during   recording sessions, increasing         artifact contamination and making            reliable

signal  recovery    a  prerequisite rather     than   an  optional   step (Barry et al.,     2003; Lenartowicz        &

Loo, 2014).


Machine      Learning      in   Cognitive      State    Detection.     Machine      learning    enables    automated

interpretation    of the complex,      high-dimensional feature sets         extracted from      preprocessed EEG

signals. Classification accuracy in EEG studies commonly ranges from 70 to 95% depending on

signal quality, dataset size, and algorithm complexity (Singh & Krishnan, 2023), establishing the


                                                          36

===== PDF PAGE 43 =====
empirical    range   within   which    Cortex's    target  of  85% classification accuracy is          situated.  Both

supervised and unsupervised approaches are established in the literature: supervised methods use

labeled   datasets    to   train  predictive     models    for   targeted    classification,   while    unsupervised

methods    identify   natural   groupings     within   neural    data  without    predefined     labels  (Kim    et  al.,

2025; Quinn      et al., 2024).   Cortex    employs a combination of both, using K-means clustering to

identify naturally occurring patterns in EEG feature space and Random Forest as the supervised

classification layer. Prakash and Poulose (2025) validate ensemble classifiers including Random

Forest  as among      the  most   effective   approaches for EEG cognitive state recognition, confirmed

through accuracy, precision, and F1-score metrics.


Clustering     Algorithms for Brain States. K-means clustering is particularly suited to Cortex's

four-state   classification    target   due   to  its  computational       efficiency    and   real-time   processing

capability (Roy     et  al., 2019;   Craik et al., 2019).      The algorithm partitions EEG           feature   vectors

into a predefined number of clusters by iteratively assigning each vector to the nearest centroid

based   on   Euclidean     distance,   updating     centroid    positions   until   cluster  assignments      stabilize

(MacQueen, 1967). In EEG studies, K=4 is an established configuration for classifying multiple

cognitive    states  simultaneously      (Lyu,    2026),   directly   matching     Cortex's    four  target   states  of

Focused, Distracted, Relaxed, and Overstimulated. Unlike hierarchical clustering approaches that

require post-hoc dendrogram analysis, K-means processes large EEG datasets quickly, making it

suitable for the near real-time adaptation requirements of an active classroom deployment.


Neural     Networks       for   EEG-Based        Systems.      While     deep   learning     architectures    such    as

Convolutional      Neural     Networks      and   Recurrent     Neural     Networks      have   demonstrated       high

classification    performance       in   EEG     research    (Lyu,    2026;    Altuwaijri     et   al.,  2025),    their

computational      demands      and   data   requirements      make     them    less  suitable    for  the  real-time,



                                                           37

===== PDF PAGE 44 =====
resource-constrained       deployment context        of Cortex.    Neural    network approaches are noted here

as part of   the  established    landscape of     EEG    classification    methods,    providing the comparative

context   within   which    Cortex's    choice   of  K-means      clustering   and Random Forest          represents a

deliberate prioritization of real-time latency and interpretability over raw classification ceiling.


         Establishing a reliable pipeline from raw EEG signal to cognitive state classification is a

necessary    condition for    Cortex but     it is not sufficient. Knowing that a student has transitioned

into a distracted or overstimulated state only becomes instructionally useful if the system knows

what to do with that information. That question, how the learning environment should respond to

detected    cognitive    states,  is  not   merely    a  software     design    problem.     It is  grounded      in  an

established body      of research    on how ADHD learners process, respond to, and are regulated by

sensory   stimuli.   Before the adaptive content logic           of Cortex can      be designed,     the  relationship

between    sensory    input   and  cognitive    regulation    in ADHD       must   be  understood. The       following

theme    examines     this  relationship    directly,   establishing    the  empirical    basis   for  why    the  type,

intensity,  and   modality    of  adaptive    stimuli   matter    as  much    as  the  speed   with   which they     are

delivered.


Sensory Regulation in Learning Environments


         For students with ADHD, the capacity to sustain learning is inseparable from the capacity

to regulate incoming sensory information. Regulatory challenges in ADHD are not peripheral to

the learning experience       but   neurobiologically      intrinsic  to  it, shaped   by the same dopaminergic

and   noradrenergic     disruptions     that  govern    attention    and   arousal.   This   theme    examines      four

regulatory    dimensions     directly   relevant   to  Cortex's design:      sensory   processing, emotional        and






                                                           38

===== PDF PAGE 45 =====
affective   regulation,    attention    and   cognitive    regulation,    and    the  intervention     strategies   that

address each within educational settings.


Concept     of  Sensory     Regulation.      Sensory     processing    in  ADHD       is  characterized     by  a  dual

vulnerability    that  defines   the  central   design   problem     Cortex    addresses.    Faraone    et  al. (2024)

establish that disruptions to dopaminergic and noradrenergic networks alter the brain's threshold

for sensory detection      and response,      meaning     ADHD learners may simultaneously over-react to

irrelevant   background      stimuli   and   under-respond      to  instructional   content    presented    at  normal

intensity.  Bijlenga    et  al. (2017)   provide    direct  empirical    evidence,    demonstrating      that atypical

sensory    profiles   including     both   hyper-sensitivity      and   hypo-sensitivity      are  core   features    of

ADHD, independent of co-occurring conditions. The practical implication is that ADHD learners

occupy    two    opposing     risk   zones   at  once:    overstimulation,      where    excessive     input   triggers

dysregulation,     and    understimulation,      where     insufficient    stimulus    intensity    fails  to  activate

engagement.     Cortex must      therefore    detect  which    zone   a student currently occupies and deliver

stimuli  calibrated    to  move    them    toward    a regulated    middle    state, the precise    function that     its

two-mode adaptive system is designed to perform. Harrison et al. (2019) and DuPaul and Stoner

(2014) both identify environmental modification as an evidence-supported intervention category,

confirming     that  deliberate    control   of  sensory    features    in a  learning    environment      can  reduce

distractibility and improve task engagement in ADHD students.


Emotional and Affective           Regulation. Emotional dysregulation is increasingly recognized as a

core  feature    of  ADHD      rather   than   a  secondary     concern.    Shaw     et al.  (2014)   document      that

between     24    and   50%     of   children     with   ADHD       exhibit    clinically    significant    emotional

dysregulation, including low         frustration tolerance      and rapid    emotional escalation,       patterns that

directly  divert   attentional   and   cognitive    resources    away    from   task  engagement. Chacko et          al.

===== PDF PAGE 46 =====
(2024)   establish that    affective states function       as moderators      of intervention effectiveness: even

well-designed instructional        content yields diminished outcomes when a student enters a session

in an elevated arousal state. This finding directly informs Cortex's Relax Mode, which activates

calming    stimuli   before    instructional    content    is  resumed     when    overstimulation      or  heightened

arousal   is  detected.    Singh   et  al.  (2025),    in  a feasibility   study   in  a  SpEd    classroom,     further

validate   that  biometric     sensing    can   provide    physiological     indicators    of  escalating    emotional

arousal   prior    to  a  behavioral     outburst,    establishing     the   technical    feasibility    of  proactive,

sensor-mediated emotional regulation support in naturalistic school environments.


Attention and Cognitive Regulation. Attentional regulation in ADHD is not simply a matter of

effort   or  motivation      but   a   structural   constraint     rooted    in  fronto-striatal    dysfunction      that

compromises inhibitory control and working memory (Faraone et al., 2024). ADHD learners rely

heavily   on   external    regulation    support    to  maintain    a  functional    focus    state  because    internal

metacognitive      scaffolding    is  impaired     (Colomer     et  al.,  2017).   This   makes     the  threshold    for

cognitive overload lower than for neurotypical peers: because baseline working memory capacity

is already    partially   consumed      by   self-regulation     demands,     instructional     designs    that  exceed

cognitive   load   limits  impair rather     than support learning (Mayer, 2014). Gkintoni et al. (2025),

synthesizing     103   studies   at  the  intersection     of  Cognitive     Load    Theory,    AI,  and   educational

neuroscience,     confirm    that  EEG-based       tools  can measure       cognitive load in real      time and that

adaptive systems informed by this data achieve significantly better learning efficiency outcomes

than  static   delivery.   This   convergence      of  cognitive    theory   and   neurotechnology        provides    the

direct scientific basis for Cortex's real-time stimulus adjustment mechanism.

Assistive Instructional Design and Multimedia Learning


         Effective instructional design for learners with ADHD extends beyond content delivery to

encompass     the  structured    presentation    of  information,     regulation    of  instructional   stimuli,   and

adaptation    to  learners'   cognitive    and   sensory   conditions.     This  theme    reviews    the  theoretical

foundations    of multimedia learning, the instructional design principles grounded in ADHD and

SpEd literature,    the  sensory properties      of learning materials as regulatory mechanisms, and the

current   landscape    of  adaptive    systems,     collectively   establishing the     design basis    for Cortex's

content architecture.


Multimedia       Learning      Theory.     The    Cognitive     Theory    of   Multimedia      Learning     (CTML),

developed and continuously refined by Mayer, provides the foundational theoretical framework

for  understanding       how    learners    process    instructional     content    delivered    through     multiple

modalities.    Mayer    (2024)    reaffirms   that  learning    is most    effective   when    visual  and   auditory

channels   are engaged      simultaneously     and   coherently,    a principle   grounded     in  the dual-channel

assumption that the cognitive system processes pictorial and verbal information through separate,



                                                          42

===== PDF PAGE 49 =====
limited-capacity      channels.    Critically,    Mayer    (2024)    identifies    generative    processing     as   the

mechanism      most responsible      for deep learning outcomes,          and argues that effective multimedia

design   must    support    this  process     without    overloading     either   channel.    Zhang    et  al.  (2024),

extending     CTML,      examined      how    the  presentation     characteristics     of  instructional    materials

influence    the  split-attention    effect,  the   cognitive    cost  incurred    when    learners   must    mentally

integrate    separated    sources    of   information.     Their    findings    demonstrate      that   variations    in

presentation     size  and   spatial   arrangement       significantly    influence    the   degree    of  extraneous

cognitive    load    experienced      during    learning    tasks,    establishing     that  spatial   and    temporal

alignment    of   visual   and   auditory    instructional    elements    is  a  critical  design    requirement     for

minimizing      unnecessary      cognitive    load    and   supporting     generative     processing.     For   ADHD

learners   whose working       memory capacity        is already reduced by self-regulation demands, these

CTML constraints are not general best practices but functional requirements: violations directly

impair the limited processing capacity available for learning.


Instructional Design for SpEd Learners. Translating multimedia learning theory into practice

for ADHD learners requires instructional frameworks that accommodate their specific cognitive,

behavioral, and regulatory characteristics. Frolli et al. (2023) demonstrated that Universal Design

for Learning (UDL), an instructional framework that emphasizes flexible and inclusive teaching

strategies tailored to learner variability, produced significantly greater improvements in reading,

writing,   and    arithmetic     performance       among     children    with    ADHD       compared      to  standard

intervention,   with   the  UDL group       showing more substantial gains across all measured learning

domains.     UDL's     three   core   principles,    multiple    means    of   engagement,      multiple    means     of

representation,    and   multiple    means    of action and expression,        translate directly into the      design

logic  of  adaptive     educational    systems:     rather  than   delivering    content    through    a single   fixed



                                                           43

===== PDF PAGE 50 =====
format,   effective    instruction   for   ADHD      learners   must    provide    varied   and   flexible   pathways

responsive    to  individual    attentional   and   cognitive    profiles.   Riaz   et  al. (2025),   in  a review    of

interaction design strategies for ADHD learning attention, identified key interface principles that

support sustained engagement, including reduced visual clutter, simplified instructional content,

step-by-step    chunked task presentation, immediate feedback loops, and predictable navigational

structures,   establishing a concrete set of digital interface specifications that operationalize UDL

principles    into  design    requirements.      Li  et  al.  (2025),    advancing     beyond     behavioral     design

principles, implemented an EEG-guided adaptive learning system for children with ADHD that

used  real-time    brainwave data to regulate cognitive control during instructional tasks, reporting

measurable      improvements        in  attention    and    self-regulation     outcomes      and   confirming      that

integrating neurophysiological monitoring with adaptive instructional delivery offers advantages

over purely behavior-based systems.


Sensory-Based       Learning      Materials.     Beyond    instructional structure,      the  sensory    properties of

learning   materials,    including    their  visual   intensity,   auditory   character,    pacing,   and   regulatory

valence,   play   a  direct  role  in determining      whether ADHD learners           sustain   engagement or       are

driven   toward     dysregulation.     Abdou     (2026),    in  a  PRISMA-compliant          systematic     review    of

sensory-interactive design strategies in early childhood learning environments for ASD, ADHD,

and   Down     Syndrome,      found    that   environmental       design   variables    including     lighting,   color,

acoustics,    spatial   configuration,      and    environmental       controllability     function    as   regulatory

dimensions     that  shape    cognitive    processing,     emotional     stability,  and   behavioral     engagement

Neuro-Adaptive Learning Systems


         The preceding themes establish both the learner profile Cortex is designed to support and

the  instructional    principles    governing     what    adaptive    content    should    look   like.  This   theme

examines     the  technological     and   theoretical   architecture    that  makes    a  state-responsive     system

possible, covering     how    adaptive learning      platforms personalize       instruction through data-driven

content adjustment, how neuro-adaptive systems extend this logic through physiological signals

and closed-loop      feedback,    and how Cognitive Load Theory defines the optimization target that

such systems must pursue.


Adaptive      Learning      Systems.     Adaptive      learning    platforms     (ALPs)     represent    educational

technologies      designed     to   individualize      the   learner's    instructional     experience     based     on

continuously      collected    performance       and    engagement       data.   Ouyang      et  al.   (2025),    in  a

comprehensive review of ALP design frameworks and real-world implementations, describe the

core mechanism: ALPs collect learner data across sessions, analyze patterns in performance and

engagement, and adjust instructional content and pathways to better match each learner's current

state  and   trajectory.   Ouyang     et  al. (2025)    identify   three   categories   of  personalization      logic:

mastery-based      pacing,   preference-based      adaptation,    and   state-based    adaptation,    which    adjusts

content   in  response    to  inferred   engagement      or  cognitive    load   states.  For  Cortex,    state-based

adaptation is the directly applicable category: rather than adapting based on quiz performance or

navigational behavior, the system adapts based on real-time EEG-classified brain states, a more

immediate     and neurologically grounded form of personalization than behavioral data alone can

provide.   The   most effective ALPs do          not  merely change      what   content    is shown    but  how    it is



                                                          47

===== PDF PAGE 54 =====
delivered,    with   adjustments      to  stimulus    format,    presentation     speed,    and   difficulty   level  all

documented as meaningful adaptive responses that yield learning outcome improvements across

diverse   educational      contexts    (Ouyang      et  al.,  2025).    For   ADHD       learners   specifically,    this

continuous recalibration is a cognitive necessity: the system must adjust the load it places on the

learner's working memory as available processing capacity fluctuates across a session, since that

buffer is already partially consumed by self-regulation demands (Sweller et al., 2011).


Neuro-Adaptive Systems. Neuro-adaptive systems extend the personalization logic of adaptive

learning   platforms    by   replacing    or  supplementing       behavioral    data   with   physiological signals,

direct  measurements        of  the   learner's   neurological      state  rather   than   inferences     drawn    from

behavioral outcomes. A foundational distinction within this field separates active BCIs, in which

users intentionally generate signals to control a system, from passive BCIs (pBCIs), in which the

system reads physiological signals continuously in the background without requiring deliberate

user   input.   Cortex    belongs     to  the   passive    category:    the   EEG     headset    reads   the   student's

brainwave     activity as   a  background process, with no action required from the learner to trigger

state classification or adaptive response. This distinction matters practically because active BCI

systems place     a  cognitive    burden    on users by     requiring intentional signal generation, which is

incompatible      with    sustained     learning     tasks.   Passive     systems,     by    contrast,    can   operate

transparently    within   a  learning session, reading states without diverting the learner's attentional

resources.   Beauchemin       et  al.  (2024)   demonstrate      this  in an educational implementation: their

closed-loop pBCI system monitored learners' real-time cognitive load via EEG and dynamically

modulated      the   speed    at   which    instructional     content     was   presented,     yielding    statistically

significant   improvements       in learning    satisfaction and      memorization task       efficacy    compared     to

fixed-pacing     conditions.    Lim    et  al. (2019),    whose     BCI-based      attention   training   program     for



                                                           48

===== PDF PAGE 55 =====
ADHD      children     demonstrated      measurable      improvements        in  attention    performance      through

EEG-driven      adaptive    task modulation,      provides an empirical precedent specifically within the

target  population,     confirming     that  physiologically      responsive     adaptive    systems     are  not  only

theoretically coherent but empirically validated in ADHD educational contexts.


         A  closed-loop adaptive system is one in which the output of a monitoring process feeds

directly  back   into  the system     as input,   creating   a  continuous     cycle  of detection,     classification,

response,    and    re-detection.     Fairclough     and    Gilleade    (2012)    formalize     this   as  the   closed

biocybernetic loop, classifying its operation into four discrete processing stages: data collection

from    the  physiological      sensor,   artifact   rectification    to  suppress    noise,   psychophysiological

inference to convert signal values into cognitive state classifications, and interface adaptation to

execute    the   instructional    response.     Critically,   the   loop   does    not   terminate     after  a  single

adaptation, it continues reading the physiological signal after each response to determine whether

the learner has     moved toward       the target state, enabling continuous recalibration throughout the

session.   Kosmyna       and    Maes    (2019)    provide     one   of   the  most    directly   relevant    empirical

implementations        of   this  architecture     through     AttentivU,      a   wearable     EEG-based       system

developed at MIT Media Lab that measures learner engagement in real time and delivers subtle

haptic   feedback      when     engagement       drops.    In  controlled     experiments,      participants     in  the

biofeedback       condition      showed      significantly      improved       task    engagement        and     higher

comprehension test performance compared to control conditions, demonstrating that closed-loop

EEG    systems     can   detect   engagement      drops    with   sufficient   reliability   to  trigger   meaningful

adaptive    responses,    and   that   a  well-timed,    appropriately      calibrated   stimulus     is sufficient    to

restore attentional engagement without requiring pedagogically elaborate intervention. Ewing et

al. (2016),    evaluating    an   EEG-driven       adaptive    game    system    built  on   a  biocybernetic      loop,



                                                           49

===== PDF PAGE 56 =====
identify feedback latency, transfer         function sensitivity, and negative feedback calibration as the

three   engineering      parameters      that   determine     whether     a   closed-loop      system    stabilizes    or

destabilizes the learner's cognitive state. These parameters define the technical requirements for

Cortex's   EEG     processing     pipeline:    state  classification    and   stimulus    adjustment     must   operate

within a latency window short enough to remain behaviorally meaningful, and the magnitude of

each   adaptive    response     must    be   calibrated    to  move     the  learner    toward    regulation    without

overshooting.


Cognitive     Load     Optimization.        Cognitive     Load    Theory,    as   operationalized      in  educational

neuroscience     research    by   Gkintoni    et  al. (2025),    provides    the  normative framework          for what

neuro-adaptive      systems    should    optimize     toward.    The   optimal    learning    state  is one   in  which

intrinsic cognitive load is matched to the learner's current processing capacity, while extraneous

cognitive    load   is   minimized      and   germane      load,   the   cognitive     effort  invested     in  schema

construction, is maximized. This is not a fixed point but a dynamic target: as available working

memory     capacity changes       across a session due to fatigue, distraction, or emotional arousal, the

instructional system must adjust the load it imposes accordingly (Sweller et al., 2011). Gkintoni

et  al.  (2025),    synthesizing      103    studies   at   the  intersection     of  CLT,     AI,   and   educational

neuroscience,     confirm that EEG-based tools can measure cognitive load states in real time with

high   accuracy,     and   that   adaptive     AI-driven     interventions     informed      by   this  data   produce

significant    gains   in  personalized      learning    efficiency.    Critically,   real-time    responsiveness      is

identified as the mechanism responsible for these gains: systems that respond to cognitive load as

it changes rather than after the fact are substantially more effective at maintaining the learner in

an  optimal    processing     state. Cognitive     overload    and   disengagement        represent   the   two failure

modes    adaptive     systems    must    prevent.    Overload     occurs    when    total   cognitive    load   exceeds



                                                           50

===== PDF PAGE 57 =====
processing capacity, typically experienced as confusion, frustration, or behavioral dysregulation;

disengagement       occurs   when    load   falls  too low,   experienced as      mind-wandering        or inattention

(Sweller    et al., 2011).    For  ADHD       learners,   the  window     between     these   two   failure   modes    is

narrower than for neurotypical peers: dopaminergic regulation atypicality means they disengage

more rapidly under low-stimulation conditions and overload more rapidly under high-stimulation

conditions, making the optimal zone both harder to reach and harder to sustain. The implication

for   Cortex     is  that    both    directions    of    regulation,     increasing     stimulus     intensity    when

disengagement      is detected    and   reducing it when       overstimulation is detected, must be built into

the  adaptive     response    logic   as   equally    essential   operations.     A   system    that   only   escalates

engagement stimuli when distraction is detected, without a corresponding capacity to de-escalate

when    overstimulation      is  detected,   would     address    only   half  the   regulatory    challenge    ADHD

learners face.


         The   literature    on  neuro-adaptive       systems     establishes    both   the   design    logic  and   the

empirical    precedent    for  Cortex's    closed-loop     adaptive    architecture. What      it does not yet fully

address   is  the  specific   input   mechanism      that  makes     the  loop   possible:   the EEG     signal   itself.

Electroencephalography (EEG) and Brain-State Monitoring


         Electroencephalography         (EEG)     is  the   primary     neurophysiological       input   mechanism

through    which    Cortex    reads   and   responds     to ADHD       learners'   cognitive    states.  This   theme

reviews    the  foundational     principles    of  EEG    signal   acquisition,    the  established     relationships

between brainwave frequency bands and cognitive states, the hardware landscape for educational

EEG    deployment,      the  technical    frameworks      for  real-time    cognitive    state  detection,    and   the

existing evidence for EEG application in educational settings.



EEG     Fundamentals.        Electroencephalography          measures     the  electrical   activity   generated    by

populations    of neurons through electrodes placed on the scalp, producing continuous waveform

data that reflects the brain's dynamic functional states across multiple frequency bands. Qin et al.

(2023),   in a  comprehensive      review of EEG acquisition and feedback technologies, describe the

evolution    of   EEG    hardware      toward    wireless,    portable,   and   non-invasive      systems    and    the

signal-processing       frameworks       used    to   transform     raw    neural    activity    into   interpretable

information,    advances that have expanded EEG application from clinical settings into cognitive

research,   brain-computer      interfaces,   and   educational    technologies.     Kumar     and Sharma (2024)

detail  the   full   pipeline    through    which     raw   scalp   electrical    signals   are   transformed     into

interpretable    cognitive-state     indicators,    covering     signal    acquisition,    preprocessing,      feature

extraction,   and   classification.    A   persistent   challenge    in  wearable     EEG    acquisition    is  signal

contamination     from movement        artifacts,  particularly    relevant   in ADHD      populations. Yang and

Lin (2023) investigated artifact mitigation strategies for wearable low-density dry EEG systems

and found that active-electrode configurations combined with Artifact Subspace Reconstruction





                                                          52

(ASR)    improved     suppression     of movement-related noise          while preserving cognitively         relevant

signal components, a finding that directly informs the preprocessing pipeline adopted in Cortex.



Brainwave      Types     and    Cognitive     States.   EEG     signals   are   decomposed       into   five  primary

frequency bands: delta (0.5 to 4 Hz), theta (4 to 8 Hz), alpha (8 to 13 Hz), beta (13 to 30 Hz), and

gamma     (30+   Hz),   each   reflecting   distinct   patterns   of neural   activity associated      with  different

cognitive   and   arousal states (Table 1).       Chikhi   et al. (2022),    in  a meta-analysis     of EEG power

spectral measures across cognitive workload studies, found that increases in frontal theta power

accompanied      by   decreases    in  alpha   power    are  consistently   associated with elevated        cognitive

workload. Among         learners with ADHD, the theta/beta            ratio (TBR)     has emerged as       one of   the

most widely studied EEG indicators of attentional regulation and dysfunction. Wang et al. (2024)

reported   that elevated TBR values were significantly associated with attentional deficits among

children   with    ADHD,      as  reflected    in  performance      measures     from    the  Conners     Continuous

Performance Test       (CPT-3), and     Picken et al. (2020)       extended    this finding    to adult populations

with   ADHD-Combined           Presentation,    confirming      that  elevated    TBR    remains    associated    with

altered cognitive processing beyond childhood. These findings support TBR as a useful indicator

of attentional functioning in ADHD while acknowledging that individual variability limits its use

as  a standalone     diagnostic    biomarker.     At  the  higher   frequency     range,   Strüber   and   Herrmann

(2022)   report   that  gamma     oscillations    are  associated    with   active   sensory   binding,    perceptual

integration,   and   higher-order     cognitive   processing,     reflecting   complex     information     processing

during cognitively demanding tasks. Taken together, variations in theta, alpha, beta, and gamma

activity,  particularly   their  relative   relationships    such   as  TBR,    provide    the  neurophysiological

EEG-based Learning Analytics


         The reviewed literature demonstrates that learning analytics has evolved from traditional

behavior    tracking   and performance monitoring toward the integration of physiological data that

can   provide    deeper    insights   into   learner   cognition.    While     conventional     systems     effectively

monitor learner activities and academic outcomes, they often lack the capability to directly assess

internal  cognitive    states  such as    attention, engagement, and concentration. EEG-based learning

analytics    addresses     this  limitation    by    enabling    real-time    monitoring      of   learner   cognitive

conditions through brainwave analysis. Existing studies have shown the effectiveness of EEG in

detecting    engagement      and    concentration,     while   multimodal      learning    analytics    systems    have

demonstrated the value of visualizing these data through dashboards and monitoring interfaces.

adaptive content delivery. Therefore, there remains a need for educational systems that not only

monitor    learner   cognitive    states   but  also   utilize  this  information     to  dynamically      personalize

learning experiences. This gap supports the development of CORTEX, which seeks to integrate

Learning Analytics        in Education. Learning analytics encompasses the collection and analysis

of  data   generated      during    learning    activities   to  understand      and   support    learner    progress.

Traditional    systems    rely  primarily    on   behavioral     indicators   such   as  attendance     records,   task

completion     rates, participation frequency,       and   interaction   logs to   evaluate learner performance

and   engagement      (Ifenthaler    &   Yau,   2020;   Viberg    et  al., 2023;    Papamitsiou     &   Economides,

2021).   While   these   systems enable educators to identify learning patterns and predict academic

outcomes, they depend heavily on observable behaviors and system-generated data that may not

accurately represent a learner's internal cognitive condition (Sharma et al., 2022). A learner may

remain   active   within   a  platform    while   experiencing significant        cognitive    distraction,   meaning

that   behavioral     indicators     and    EEG-derived        indicators     capture    fundamentally        different

dimensions     of  engagement (Moon et al.,          2022). This     limitation has    led  researchers to explore

physiological     data  sources,    including    EEG    signals,   as  complements       to  conventional     learning

analytics   to provide    deeper insight into student learning states (Apicella et al., 2022; Sharma et

al., 2022).   Modern      frameworks      increasingly    integrate    multiple   data   sources,   and   multimodal

learning   analytics   systems     that  combine behavioral tracking with physiological measurements

have demonstrated improved accuracy in learner-state detection (Becerra et al., 2023; Becerra et

al., 2025).

through platform interaction         metrics, but its limitations become pronounced in ADHD contexts

alone   cannot reveal the cognitive factors influencing             student   success or    failure:   a learner   may

achieve   satisfactory performance        despite high     cognitive workload,        while another     may    struggle

performance     monitoring frameworks provides information about cognitive states that academic

records cannot     capture,   and such     integration has     been proposed as a mechanism for improving

the  accuracy     of  educational     analytics    and   supporting     adaptive    learning    systems    capable    of

EEG Data Visualization. The increasing use of EEG in educational research has created a need

for visualization methods that transform raw brainwave data into interpretable representations of

learner   attention,   engagement,       and   cognitive    workload      accessible    to  educators     during    live

sessions (Casson, 2021; Apicella et al., 2022; Samimisabet et al., 2025). Visual dashboards have

demonstrated     that integrating physiological and behavioral information into real-time interfaces

allows educators to better understand learner experiences and support personalized interventions

(Becerra    et  al., 2023).   EEG     visualization    thus   serves    as  a  critical  bridge   between     complex

neurological    data   and   actionable    educational    insight, enabling      teachers   to  move    from   reactive

behavioral    observation toward       proactive, data-informed        instructional decision-making (Casson,

2021;   Samimisabet      et  al., 2025).   Apicella    et  al. (2022) developed an        EEG-based engagement

detection   system    using wearable EEG         devices and machine learning that            identified changes in

cognitive   and   emotional     engagement      with   sufficient   accuracy     to support    adaptive   educational

applications,    and   Zhuo    et  al. (2025)    achieved     high   classification    accuracy    in  distinguishing

===== PDF PAGE 66 =====
concentration     states  during    online   learning   using   a  wearable     EEG    headband,     confirming that

real-time monitoring of learner attention is technically feasible in naturalistic learning conditions.

Becerra    et al.  (2023),   in  the  M2LADS system, integrated             EEG-based      attention measurements

into  multimodal      learning    analytics    dashboards      alongside    behavioral     and    performance      data,

demonstrating      that   such    visualization    supports    both    real-time    monitoring     and    post-session

analysis.   These   findings    collectively    inform    the  design   of  Cortex's    teacher   dashboard,     which

displays   real-time    EEG-classified       cognitive    states   and   engagement      indicators    as  actionable,

interpretable information rather than raw signal data, enabling teachers to monitor and intervene

with precision that behavioral observation alone cannot provide.



                                        Synthesis of Related Literature

         The    literature   reviewed     across    eight   themes     converges     on   a   single,   compounding

problem: the cognitive states of students with ADHD are neurophysiologically dynamic, largely

invisible    to  behavioral      observation,     and    inadequately      served    by    the   static  instructional

environments currently available          in  Philippine    Special Education       settings. Theme I established

the legislative and curricular framework            within   which    this problem is situated, confirming that

while DepEd's Transition Curriculum mandates individualized, functional instruction for learners

with disabilities,    the  tools available to implement that mandate in real time remain insufficient.

Theme     II  deepened      this  gap    by   establishing    that   ADHD      learning    is  state-dependent      and

affectively   modulated,     meaning     that   attentional   capacity    fluctuates   continuously      in ways    that

behavioral    observation cannot       reliably   detect  or respond     to. Theme     III demonstrated that EEG

preprocessing and machine learning-based classification provide a technically validated pathway

for  detecting    these   cognitive     state  fluctuations    objectively    and   in  real   time,   with   K-means




                                                           60

===== PDF PAGE 67 =====
clustering   and   Random      Forest   identified as    appropriate    methods for      a four-state   classification

target in a classroom-deployable system.

         Themes IV and V addressed what the system should do once a cognitive state is detected.

Theme     IV   established    that  ADHD       learners   occupy     two   simultaneous      regulatory    risk  zones,

overstimulation and understimulation, and that effective intervention must be capable of moving

a learner    toward    a  regulated    middle    state  through    deliberate,   individually     calibrated   sensory

design.   Theme     V  established     the instructional    design   principles that     govern    what   that content

should    look   like,  grounding     Cortex's    multimedia      content   architecture     in  CTML,     UDL,     and

sensory-based     design research, and identifying a persistent gap in existing adaptive systems: no

current   tool   combines      real-time     EEG     integration,    ADHD-specific         adaptive    delivery,    and

teacher-mediated oversight within a structured SpEd classroom context. Theme VI resolved the

architectural question by establishing the closed biocybernetic loop as the design framework for

Cortex's   adaptive    engine,    confirming     through    Cognitive    Load    Theory    that   both  directions    of

regulation, escalation when disengagement is detected and de-escalation when overstimulation is

detected, must be built into the system as equally essential operations.

         Themes VII and VIII addressed the neurophysiological and analytical foundations of the

teacher-facing dimension        of Cortex.     Theme VII      confirmed that wearable consumer-grade EEG

devices   can reliably capture the frequency-band data necessary for cognitive state classification

in  naturalistic   settings,   and   that  the   theta/beta   ratio   and   related   spectral   patterns   provide    a

neurophysiologically        grounded      basis   for   Cortex's    four   target   states:   Focused,     Distracted,

Relaxed, and Overstimulated.          Theme VIII       established that    existing learning analytics systems,

while   effective   at  tracking   behavioral     and  performance indicators, cannot           capture   the  internal

cognitive   conditions that     determine whether a student is genuinely engaged or silently drifting,




                                                           61

===== PDF PAGE 68 =====
and that EEG-based visualization integrated into a teacher-facing dashboard addresses precisely

this gap by transforming neurophysiological data into actionable, real-time instructional insight.

Taken    together,   the  literature   does   not   merely    support    Cortex   as  a  plausible    intervention.    It

identifies Cortex as the necessary response to a convergence of documented gaps that no existing

system     addresses      in   combination:       real-time     EEG-based        cognitive     state   classification,

neuro-adaptive      multimedia      delivery    aligned    with   the  DepEd      Transition    Curriculum,      and   a

teacher    monitoring      interface    that   replaces     reactive    behavioral     inference     with   objective,

continuous    neurophysiological data. The methodology that follows describes how each of these

components is designed, developed, and evaluated within a structured Special Education context.

Research Design

         This   study   will  adopt   a  quantitative    research    design   [to  systematically     investigate the

effectiveness     of   the  EEG-integrated        neuro-adaptive       multimedia      system     using   measurable

brainwave     data,  signal   processing     outputs,    classification    performance      metrics,   and   objective

usability   evaluation     techniques     for  cognitive    state  recognition     and   ADHD-focused         learning

support   (Craik et al., 2019; Roy et al., 2019). The study will focus on collecting and analyzing

numerical     data    derived    from     EEG     processing,     brain-state    classification,     and   user-based

evaluation to assess the performance of Cortex.


         While     the   study    primarily     employs      a  quantitative     approach      to  evaluate     system

performance      and   usability,   it is  more   appropriately      characterized     as  developmental      research

employing quantitative evaluation. This is because the study focuses on the design, development,



                                                           62

===== PDF PAGE 69 =====
refinement,     and   validation     of   an   EEG-integrated       neuro-adaptive       multimedia      system,     with

quantitative    methods      used    to  assess    its functionality,     classification    performance,       and   user

acceptance rather than to test causal relationships or hypotheses.

         EEG data      will  undergo    preprocessing      to  remove noise      and   artifacts  such as eye blinks,

muscle movements, and environmental interference. After preprocessing, feature extraction will

be  performed       to  transform     preprocessed       EEG    signals    into   meaningful      representations      for

classification.    These     extracted    features    may     include    time-domain,       frequency-domain,         and

wavelet-based      characteristics     that  capture    different   aspects    of  neural   activity.   The   processed

features   will   then   be   used   as   inputs   for   the  classification     model    to   identify   cognitive     or

attentional states.


         The brain-state classification model will be evaluated using standard performance metrics

such   as  accuracy,    precision,    recall,   and   F1-score.    These    metrics    will  determine     the  model’s

ability  to  correctly   classify   EEG-based       cognitive    states   relevant   to  ADHD learning         behavior.

Confusion      matrix    analysis    will   also   be   applied    to  further    examine     correct    and   incorrect

predictions and evaluate classification reliability.


         The   usability and effectiveness of         the  student and     teacher applications will be assessed

using   structured    Likert-scale    questionnaires     and rating     scales. These instruments         will  measure

indicators   such   as  ease   of use,   interface clarity,    engagement, responsiveness,           adaptability,    and

overall system acceptability. Feedback from users will be used to determine how effectively the

system supports learning activities and classroom monitoring of attentional states.

Ethical Considerations


         Because Cortex collects and acts on real-time neurophysiological data from minors with

ADHD,      a population     already   recognized     in  Philippine law as       requiring heightened protection,

this  study's   ethical  framework      is  built  around    three   converging     obligations:    safeguarding      the

student   as a  data subject and as a child, ensuring the comfort and autonomy of the student as a

research   participant,    and   constraining     how   the   adults  granted    access    to this  data,  namely     the

teachers, may use it.




                                                           67

===== PDF PAGE 74 =====
         The   brainwave and       behavioral data      Cortex    collects   falls squarely within what Republic

Act   No.   10173,    the   Data   Privacy    Act    of  2012,    classifies   as  sensitive   personal    information:

Section   3(l)  of the Act extends this classification to any information concerning an individual's

health    or   education,      and     EEG-derived        cognitive      state   data    satisfies    both    categories

simultaneously,      since   it is generated     within   a  school    setting but reflects a      neurophysiological

condition. Under       Section 13     of the Act, sensitive      personal    information may only be processed

under stricter conditions       than   ordinary personal data, generally requiring explicit consent and a

clearly  declared, legitimate purpose, and the Act's general principles of transparency, legitimate

purpose, and proportionality under Section 11 require that the data Cortex collects be limited to

what its classification      function actually      needs and be processed only for that stated purpose. In

practice,   this  means     the  study    will  store   EEG     logs   and   classification    outputs    in  encrypted,

access-controlled storage, retain them            only   for the duration     necessary to complete the research

and any required       validation, and     never use     raw or    identifiable   brainwave data for any purpose

beyond     the   system's    adaptive     classification     function.    The    National     Privacy    Commission's

Advisory No. 2024-03, the Guidelines on Child-Oriented Transparency, reinforces this further: it

defines   a  "child"   as  anyone     below    eighteen,    or  anyone    over   eighteen    unable    to fully protect

themselves due to a physical or mental disability, a definition that places every participant in this

study,   regardless    of  exact   age,   under    its  highest   tier  of  protection,     and   it requires   that  any

heightened-risk      processing     of  a  child's  data   involve    parental   or  guardian     participation    in  the

decision   rather than    relying on     the  child's own consent. The          feasibility of ethically monitoring

ADHD      learners' physiological signals in this          way is not without precedent; Singh et al. (2025)

demonstrated      the   viability   of   using   biometric     sensors    to  detect   physiological      precursors     to

behavioral    outbursts    in  children    with   ADHD,      while   Herrero-Martín et al. (2026)          developed      a

multilayered     data-logging     framework      for  multimodal      physiological     and   behavioral    signals   in

another neurodevelopmental population, both offering working models for handling this category

of data responsibly.


         Beyond data handling, the physical and psychological comfort of the student in wearing

the EEG device is treated as a non-negotiable condition of participation rather than a secondary

concern.   ADHD and other neurodevelopmental profiles are frequently accompanied by atypical

student   will  be  made to    wear   the  headset against their will at any point in a session, refusal or

removal     of   the   device     carries   no   academic      or   behavioral     consequence,       and   continued

participation in the study remains entirely voluntary and revocable at any time without affecting

the student's standing in PMSI's SNED program.

Software Methodology




















                                    Figure 2. Modified Prototyping SDLC

         This   study   adopts   a  Modified    Prototyping     Software     Development       Life  Cycle   (SDLC)

methodology for       the  development of      Cortex. The modified approach            was selected because the

study   involves    the   integration    of   EEG     technology,     cognitive    state  classification,    adaptive

multimedia,     and  user-centered     interfaces,   all of  which    require iterative    design,   validation, and

refinement    before   full-scale   implementation.       Developing     an  EEG-assisted      educational     system

entails  evaluating    not  only   software    functionality    but  also  the  reliability  of signal processing,

cognitive    state   modeling,     multimedia      adaptation,     and    user   interaction.    Through     iterative

prototyping,    the  researchers    can   continuously assess and        improve these      components based        on

testing results and stakeholder feedback while minimizing the risks associated with developing a

complex and novel system.


         Compared      with    other   SDLC      models,    the   Modified     Prototyping      approach     is  more

appropriate    for the objectives of      this study.   Unlike   the  Waterfall   model, which       follows a rigid




                                                          72

===== PDF PAGE 79 =====
sequential   process with     limited flexibility for     revisions,   prototyping accommodates            changes as

new   findings    emerge     during    system    validation.    While    Agile   emphasizes      rapid,   incremental

software    releases   and   continuous     feature   delivery,   this  study   focuses   primarily    on   refining   a

research    prototype    rather   than   deploying    production-ready       software.    Similarly,    although    the

Spiral  model     provides    extensive    risk  management        for  large-scale    and  high-cost    projects,   its

complexity and resource requirements exceed the needs of this research. Therefore, the Modified

Prototyping    SDLC     provides    an effective    balance    between structured development and iterative

refinement,    making     it well   suited   for  validating    the   functionality,   usability,   and   educational

effectiveness of the proposed EEG-integrated adaptive learning system.

functional   and technical requirements needed            for the   development of       Cortex.   Information     was

collected   through     a review    of   related   literature  and   studies   to  establish    the  theoretical    and

technical foundation of the system. Interviews were also conducted with relevant stakeholders to

obtain insights regarding the needs, challenges, and expectations associated with EEG-integrated

adaptive learning applications for students with ADHD. The gathered information served as the

basis for defining the system requirements and the features included in the system’s three major

modules.     Existing     adaptive    learning     systems,     EEG-based       educational      technologies,      and

ADHD-focused        learning    interventions    were    also  examined     to  identify   effective   practices   and

potential   implementation      strategies.   The   results   of  this  phase guided the planning         and   design

decisions   for  the  succeeding      stages  of  development.      Furthermore,      the  identified requirements

established    the   criteria  that  would     later  be   used   to  evaluate    the  system's    effectiveness     and

         EEG Data Collection. The EEG data collection phase will involve gathering brainwave

dataset will provide the foundation for understanding the relationship between EEG patterns and

characteristics    from the    EEG signals that can contribute to accurate cognitive state recognition.

Data   normalization      and  organization     procedures will also be         performed to ensure        consistency

across   all samples.    Through preprocessing,         the  dataset   will  become     more    suitable for machine

learning applications and will help improve the reliability of subsequent classification results.

Data Gathering Tools

         EEG     Headband.      An    electroencephalography        (EEG)     headband     will  be  utilized   as  the

primary   tool for   collecting brainwave data from participants. The device will be equipped with

non-invasive sensors positioned on specific areas of the scalp to detect and record the electrical

activity  generated     by  the   brain.  During     the  data  collection    process,   the  EEG     headband     will

continuously     capture    neural    signals   associated     with   cognitive    functions    such    as  attention,

concentration, and mental engagement. The collected EEG data will serve as the foundation for

analyzing brainwave patterns and developing the classification model used in the study.

         As of   the  preparation of this study, the final EEG headband to be used has not yet been

determined.     The    researchers     are  currently    evaluating     several   commercially       available    EEG

devices,   including    the  Muse    2  Headband      (Interaxon    Inc.),  Emotiv    Insight   (Emotiv Inc.),     and

OpenBCI      Ultracortex    Mark     IV  with    Cyton    Board    (OpenBCI).      These    devices   were    selected

because   they are widely used        in  EEG    research and     provide distinct advantages for educational

and cognitive monitoring applications. They offer non-invasive brain signal acquisition, software

development support, and varying levels of signal quality, portability, and channel configuration,

making     them    suitable   candidates     for   real-time    cognitive    state  classification    in   a  learning

environment. Additionally, each device has been utilized in previous studies involving attention

monitoring, neurofeedback, or brain-computer interface applications, supporting their relevance

for the proposed system.







                                                          91

===== PDF PAGE 98 =====
The   final  EEG     device    will  be   selected   based    on  predefined     technical    and   practical   criteria,

including    signal quality, number of        EEG     channels, sampling       rate, ease of    use, comfort     during

prolonged     use   by   learners,   software     compatibility,     availability    of  application    programming

interfaces   (APIs)    or  software     development      kits  (SDKs),     cost,   and   overall   suitability   for  the

objectives    of  the   study.   These    criteria  ensure    that  the   selected   device    can   reliably   support

real-time   EEG     acquisition    while    remaining     practical   for  classroom-based       implementation.       A

detailed   comparison       of  the   candidate     devices,    including    their   specifications,     features,   and

corresponding images, is presented in Appendix E.


         Bluetooth      Connectivity.      Bluetooth     technology      will  be   utilized   to  establish   wireless

communication between the EEG headband and the data collection device. This connection will

enable the real-time transmission of EEG signals without the need for physical cables, allowing

participants    to  move    comfortably      during    the  data   collection    sessions.   The    use  of  Bluetooth

connectivity     will  facilitate   efficient   data   transfer   and   support    the  continuous      acquisition    of

brainwave data throughout the study.


         EEG Acquisition Software. Companion software provided with the EEG headband will

be utilized   to  receive,   visualize,   and   record   the brainwave      signals transmitted from the device.

This   software    will   interface    directly   with   the   headband      through    the   established    Bluetooth

connection,    converting raw       electrical signals into structured        digital data   that can be stored       for

further use. During data collection, the software will display real-time signal output, allowing the

researcher to monitor       the  quality   and stability    of the connection throughout           each session. The

acquisition software will serve as the primary medium through which raw EEG data is captured

and prepared for storage.

    1.   Band Pass Filters

              ○   A   band-pass     filter  will   be  applied    to  the   raw   EEG     signal   to  retain   only   the

                  frequency      ranges    that   are   relevant    to  cognitive    processing      while   discarding

                  frequencies that fall outside this range.

              ○   Brainwave      activity associated with attention, relaxation, and mental engagement

                  typically    occurs    within   a  specific   frequency     band,    and   any  signal   outside    this

                  range is generally considered irrelevant or noise.

              ○   By isolating this meaningful frequency range, the band-pass filter ensures that the

                  EEG     signal   used   for  further   analysis is   concentrated on       the  brain activity    most

                  relevant     to   the   cognitive     states    being    studied,    namely      focus,    relaxation,

                  distraction, and overstimulation.

              ○   Expected Output: EEG signal filtered to retain only the relevant frequency bands

                  for cognitive state analysis.

    2.   Notch Filters

              ○   A   notch    filter  will   be   used   to  remove      a  very   specific   and    narrow    band    of

                  frequency     that corresponds       to electrical interference from nearby power sources,

                  such    as  electrical   wiring,    lighting   fixtures,   or   other   electronic    devices    in  the

                  testing environment.

              ○   This   type   of  interference     is extremely     common in EEG          recordings because        the

                  electrical   signals   from   the  surrounding environment can             easily be   picked up     by

                  the sensitive sensors on the EEG headband.




                                                           103

===== PDF PAGE 110 =====
             ○    Without removing this interference, the resulting brainwave readings may appear

                  noisy or distorted, making it more difficult to accurately detect the subtle changes

                  in brain activity that correspond to different cognitive states.

             ○    The   notch   filter  specifically   targets   and   eliminates    this  narrow    frequency band

                  without affecting the rest of the meaningful EEG signal.

             ○    Expected Output: EEG signal with electrical power-line interference removed.

    3.   Independent Component Analysis (ICA)

             ○    Independent      Component       Analysis    will  be  utilized   as  a  more   advanced     artifact

                  removal     technique    to  separate    genuine    brain   activity   from   artifacts  caused    by

                  physical    movements      made by     the  participant   during data collection, such as eye

                  blinking, eye movement, jaw clenching, or slight shifts in posture.

             ○    These    types of   movements       generate   their own distinct electrical signals that can

                  overlap    with   and  contaminate      the  actual   brainwave     data,  making     it difficult  to

                  distinguish between true neural activity and movement-related noise.

             ○    ICA     works    by   mathematically        decomposing       the   EEG     signal   into   multiple

                  independent     components, each        representing a different       underlying source of       the

                  signal.

             ○    Once    these   components       are  separated,    the  ones    identified   as  artifacts   can  be

                  removed,     while the components         representing    genuine brain activity       are retained

                  and reconstructed into a cleaner version of the original signal.

             ○    Expected      Output:     EEG    signal   with  movement-related        artifacts  removed while

                  preserving genuine brain activity






                                                          104

===== PDF PAGE 111 =====
    4.   Epoching

             ○    Following the      removal of artifacts and noise, the continuous EEG recording will

                  be segmented into smaller,          fixed-length    time   windows through a process known

                  as epoching.

             ○    This step is    necessary     because cognitive states       are  not  static;  they can shift and

                  change    throughout     a  single   data  collection    session   as  participants    engage    with

                  different types of content or experience varying levels of mental engagement.

             ○    By    dividing    the   EEG     recording    into   smaller    segments,     each   epoch     can   be

                  analyzed     individually      and   matched     to   the   specific   cognitive     state  that   the

                  participant was experiencing during that particular moment in the session.

             ○    This allows the study to capture more precise and time-relevant patterns in brain

                  activity, which is essential for accurately training and evaluating the classification

                  model used to detect focus, relaxation, distraction, and overstimulation.

             ○    Expected      Output:     Segmented      EEG epochs       prepared for     feature   extraction and

                  classification.

    5.   Quality Assessment

             ○    The   preprocessed      EEG    data will undergo      a  quality   assessment to verify that all

                  preprocessing procedures have been successfully applied.

             ○    The    researcher    will  inspect    the  processed     EEG     signals   to  determine     whether

                  unwanted      noise,   power-line     interference,    and   movement-related        artifacts   have

                  been sufficiently reduced while preserving the meaningful brainwave activity.

             ○    Signal quality metrics and visual inspection of the processed EEG recordings will

                  be performed to evaluate the effectiveness of the preprocessing pipeline.




                                                          105

             ○    EEG recordings that fail to meet the established quality criteria will be reviewed

                  and   reprocessed,     when    necessary,    to  ensure    consistency     and   reliability  before

                  proceeding to feature extraction.

             ○    Expected Output: Quality-assessed and validated preprocessed EEG dataset that

                  satisfies  the  established    preprocessing     criteria and is ready for feature extraction

                  and classification.

    1.   Power Spectral Density (PSD)

             ○    Power Spectral Density (PSD) estimation using Welch's Method will be employed

                  to analyze the frequency content of each preprocessed EEG epoch.

             ○    Rather than examining the EEG signal solely in the time domain, PSD transforms

                  the  signal  into the   frequency domain,       allowing the distribution of       signal   power

                  across different frequency bands to be measured.

             ○    Welch's Method improves the stability            and reliability of    the power estimates by

                  dividing    each   EEG     epoch    into  overlapping     segments,     computing      the  power





                                                        117

===== PDF PAGE 124 =====
                  spectrum of each segment using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), and averaging

                  the resulting spectra.

             ○    This   approach     reduces    the  effect   of  random     noise   and   produces    a  smoother

                  estimate    of  the  EEG     power    spectrum,    making     it well   suited   for  EEG    signal

                  analysis.

             ○    Expected Output: Power Spectral Density estimates for each preprocessed EEG

                  epoch.

    2.   Feature Extraction

             ○    Following the computation of the Power Spectral Density, feature extraction will

                  be  performed     by  obtaining    the  average    band   power values from the theta (4–8

                  Hz), alpha (8–13 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz), and gamma (30–40 Hz) frequency bands.

             ○    These    band    power     values   represent     the  relative   amount     of   neural   activity

                  occurring     within    each   frequency      range   and    provide    a   compact     numerical

                  representation of the participant's brain activity during each multimedia stimulus.

             ○    Rather   than   utilizing thousands     of raw    voltage   samples    collected   throughout     an

                  EEG    recording,    each   epoch   is summarized      into  four representative band        power

                  features.

             ○    This process reduces data dimensionality while preserving the frequency-domain

                  characteristics    most   relevant    to  cognitive   state   analysis,   making    the  extracted

                  features suitable for subsequent normalization, clustering, and supervised machine

                  learning.

             ○    Expected     Output:     Extracted EEG       band   power features      (theta,  alpha, beta,   and

                  gamma).




                                                         118

===== PDF PAGE 125 =====
    3.   Feature Scaling / Z-score Normalization

              ○   Feature    scaling will    be applied to the extracted EEG band power values prior to

                  clustering.

              ○   Z-score    normalization     will  standardize each       feature by    subtracting    its mean and

                  dividing it by its standard deviation.

              ○   Standardization       ensures     that   alpha,    beta,   theta,   and   gamma       band    powers

                  contribute equally during clustering regardless of their original numerical ranges.

              ○   This prevents features with naturally larger values from dominating the clustering

                  process.

              ○   Expected      Output:     Standardized      EEG     feature   dataset   with   normalized     feature

                  values.

    4.   Dataset Preparation

              ○   Following EEG preprocessing and feature extraction, the extracted EEG features

                  will be organized into a structured dataset for clustering analysis.

              ○   Each    row   of  the  dataset   will  represent    a  participant or    EEG    recording session,

                  while each column will contain the extracted EEG features.

              ○   The   dataset   will   be  reviewed     to  ensure   that all  required features      are  complete,

                  consistently formatted, and suitable for analysis.

              ○   Since clustering is an unsupervised learning technique, no predefined class labels

                  will be assigned during this stage.

              ○   Expected      Output:      Complete      and    organized     EEG     feature   dataset    ready   for

                  clustering.

    5.   Determining the Optimal Number of Clusters

             ○    The   optimal   number of clusters will be determined using the Elbow Method and

             ○    Multiple cluster counts will be evaluated to determine the quality of the resulting

                  minimal improvement, while the Silhouette Score will measure cluster separation.

             ○    This process will verify whether the EEG data naturally forms four cognitive-state

                  clusters.

             ○    Expected Output: Optimal number of clusters identified for K-Means clustering.

    6.   K-Means Clustering

             ○    K-Means      clustering    will   be   applied    using   the   optimal    number      of  clusters

                  identified in the previous step.

             ○    The   algorithm    will  iteratively assign    EEG    feature   vectors   to the  nearest cluster

                  centroid and update the centroids until convergence.

             ○    EEG    recordings    with   similar   frequency-domain       characteristics    will  be  grouped

                  together.

         Mean.     The   mean     will  be  calculated    for  each    extracted   EEG     feature   to  determine     its

average value across all observations in the dataset. It serves as the central reference point during

z-score normalization, allowing each feature to be centered by subtracting its average value. This

helps  standardize     the  data   and   ensures    consistency     across   the  extracted    EEG    features    before

clustering.








                                         Equation 15. Variance Formula


         Variance. Variance will be calculated to quantify the spread of the extracted EEG feature

values   around their     mean. It represents the       average squared deviation of the observations from

the mean and serves as the basis for computing the standard deviation. Although variance is not

directly  used in    the z-score normalization formula,            it provides   a  measure of feature variability

that contributes to the calculation of the standard deviation required for standardization.

         Standard Deviation.         The standard deviation will be computed for each extracted EEG

feature   to  measure     the  amount     of   variation   or  dispersion    of  the   feature   values   from    their

corresponding     mean. During z-score normalization, the standard deviation is used to scale each

feature so that    all EEG band power         values   have   a  comparable range, preventing features with

larger numerical values from having a greater influence on the clustering process.




                                         Equation 17. Z-score Formula


         Z-Score Normalization. Z-score normalization will be used to standardize the extracted

EEG    features   before   clustering    by  transforming     each   feature   based   on its mean     and   standard

placed    on   a  common       scale,   preventing     features   with    larger   numerical     magnitudes      from

disproportionately     influencing     the  distance   calculations    performed     by the   K-Means      clustering

algorithm.

Data Gathering Tools


         Cortex    Application      & Cognitive       State Classifier. This serves         as the central    software

environment     in which the trained Random Forest              model processes       incoming EEG feature data

and   assigns   the   learner's   current    state  to  one    of  the  four   target   classes:   focused,    relaxed,

distracted,   or  overstimulated.     This   classification    output   is  what determines which multimedia

adaptation, if any, is triggered next.


         Adaptive     Content     Engine.     This   module    holds    the  predefined    mapping between         each

classified   cognitive    state  and  its  corresponding      multimedia response,        such as    adjusting visual

complexity, pacing, or auditory stimulation. It is responsible for translating a classification result

into a concrete change in what the learner sees or hears.


         System      Analytics     Logger.      This   automated       backend     module     records     timestamps,

classified states, and the specific multimedia adaptation               triggered    at each point    in the   session.

This  log   is what    subsequent     evaluation,    and   refinement     work   will draw     on to judge whether

adaptations were appropriate and well-timed.
